# Visual Style

## Direction

Create a polished vertical teaching short that feels like an instructor moving a camera across a large classroom slide, with brief glossy motion-graphic inserts whenever the slide needs more explanation.

## Palette

- Canvas: warm off-white and pale blue tech haze.
- Primary teaching blue: `#3B82F6`.
- Token pink: `#EC4899`.
- Attention orange: `#F97316`.
- Ink: deep slate for readable labels.

## Slide Treatment

- Keep the original token slide visually authoritative.
- Use a full-height opening scan so viewers understand the horizontal slide structure.
- Use full-slide overview states where both left and right edges are visible.
- Use deliberate close-ups for the sentence, token chips, context-window card, and practical scale card.
- Add cursor moves, focus rectangles, and low underlines only when the narration names a target.

## Motion Graphics

- Use frosted-glass cards with translucent layered fills, rim highlights, inner shine, soft shadows, and 3D rotation.
- Use connected token-flow diagrams instead of decorative empty boxes.
- Token cards should feel physical: tiny z-depth entrances, float, and smooth handoff into a memory tray.
- Keep the AI-tech background light, quiet, and deterministic.

## Avatar And Captions

- Use compact portrait PiP during detailed slide close-ups.
- Use a wider rectangular avatar panel when the slide is zoomed out or during recap.
- During CTA, enlarge the avatar close to full canvas width and keep captions in the clear band above it.
- Captions are text-only with outlined inactive words and medium solid blue active words.

## Avoid

- Do not redesign the source slide.
- Do not use a dark neon grid.
- Do not cover slide text with captions, avatar, or motion cards.
- Do not claim the visible slide has exactly seven chips, because the source image shows six chips.
