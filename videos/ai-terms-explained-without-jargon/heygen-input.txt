AI words sound complicated. They're mostly just normal ideas wearing a lab coat.

AI means software that can do tasks we usually associate with people, like writing, seeing, sorting, or deciding.

A model is the engine. It has learned patterns from tons of examples.

An L L M, or large language model, is a model that predicts words really well. That's why it can chat.

A prompt is your instruction. Better prompt, better answer. Like giving directions to a very fast intern.

Tokens are tiny chunks of text. Not always full words. The AI reads and writes in tokens.

The context window is the AI's short-term memory. If it gets full, older stuff falls out.

Training data is what helped teach the model. Inference is when it uses what it learned to answer you.

A hallucination is when AI sounds confident but gets the facts wrong. Basically: fluent nonsense.

Embeddings turn meaning into numbers, so AI can find ideas that are similar, not just exact words.

RAG means retrieval augmented generation. The AI checks outside sources before answering, like open-book test mode.

An agent is AI that can take steps: search, click, write, check, and keep going toward a goal.

That's the cheat sheet. AI is less magic when the words stop being weird.
