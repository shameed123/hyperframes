## Expanded Explainer Script

AI words sound complicated, but most of them are normal ideas wearing a lab coat.

AI means software that can do tasks we usually associate with people. It can write, recognize images, sort information, make recommendations, or help decide what to do next.

A model is the engine inside the AI. It has learned patterns from lots of examples, so when you give it something new, it can make a useful prediction or response.

An LLM, or large language model, is a model built for language. It predicts what words should come next, which is why it can chat, summarize, explain, brainstorm, and write.

A prompt is your instruction to the AI. It is the question, task, or direction you type in. A vague prompt usually gets a vague answer. A clear prompt gives the model a much better target.

Tokens are the little chunks of text the AI reads and writes. A token might be a whole word, part of a word, a number, or punctuation. Models do not see text exactly like we do. They process it in tokens.

The context window is the AI's short-term memory for the current conversation. It is how much information the model can keep in view at once. When the window fills up, older details can become harder for the AI to use.

Training data is the material used to teach the model before you ever talk to it. It helps the model learn patterns in language, code, images, facts, and examples.

Inference is what happens when the model uses what it learned to answer you right now. Training is the learning phase. Inference is the answering phase.

A hallucination is when AI sounds confident but gets the facts wrong. The sentence may be smooth, but the information can still be false, so important answers need checking.

Embeddings turn meaning into numbers. That lets AI compare ideas by similarity, not just exact matching. It is how a system can understand that "car" and "vehicle" are related even if the words are different.

RAG means retrieval augmented generation. In plain English, it means the AI looks up reference material before answering. Think of it like open-book test mode: first it retrieves useful sources, then it writes the answer.

An agent is AI that can take steps toward a goal. Instead of only answering once, it can search, click, write, check its work, and keep going through a task.

That is the cheat sheet. AI gets a lot less magical once the words stop being weird. Like, subscribe, and follow for more plain-English AI breakdowns.
