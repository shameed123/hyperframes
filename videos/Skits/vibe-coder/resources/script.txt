I’m a senior software engineer.

I’ve spent years learning databases, security, testing, deployment, and the ancient ritual of staring at an error message until it feels guilty.

But last Tuesday, a guy in a hoodie opened his laptop and said:

“Today, I’m building a billion-dollar SaaS app using AI.”

I said, “Cool. What does it do?”

He said, “It disrupts.”

I said, “Disrupts what?”

He said, “The market.”

I said, “Which market?”

He paused.

Then said, “Yes.”

That’s when I knew we had entered the consultant dimension.

He called his method vibe coding.

Vibe coding is when you describe the emotional atmosphere of software to AI and hope capitalism fills in the blanks.

He typed:

“Build me a SaaS app like Uber, Airbnb, Shopify, Netflix, and ChatGPT… but for dentists. Add dark mode.”

The AI replied:

“Sure. Here is a login page.”

He leaned back like he had discovered fire.

“We’re basically funded.”

I looked at the screen.

Username field. Password field. Blue button.

That was the whole company.

I said:

“That’s not a SaaS app. That’s a form with anxiety.”

Then the app broke.

The screen said:

Cannot read properties of undefined.

I said:

“That’s JavaScript saying, ‘I have no father.’”

He typed:

“Fix this error. Do not change anything.”

The AI responded:

“I have rewritten your entire app structure.”

It deleted authentication.

He said:

“Security through absence.”

I said:

“That is not security. That is a missing door.”

Then I asked, “Where are you storing user data?”

He said:

“Local storage.”

I said, “For a SaaS app?”

He said, “It’s lightweight.”

I said, “So if the user clears their browser cache, their entire business disappears?”

He said:

“Built-in privacy.”

That’s not privacy.

That’s a magic trick with lawsuits.

Then came security.

He asked AI to make it secure.

The AI added a giant lock emoji to the header.

🔒

He said:

“Enterprise-grade.”

I said:

“That is punctuation with confidence.”

Then came the investor demo.

Bold choice.

The app had no login, no database, no working dashboard, and security provided by an emoji.

The investor asked:

“What problem does this solve?”

The vibe coder said:

“It uses AI.”

The investor said, “Okay, but what problem?”

He said:

“The problem of not using AI.”

The investor clicked Get Started.

Immediately, his phone buzzed.

“Why did it charge my card four thousand dollars?”

I said, “Why is billing before onboarding?”

The vibe coder said:

“Revenue should come before confusion.”

I asked, “Where’s the refund button?”

He said:

“We removed it to improve conversion.”

I said:

“Conversion into what? A defendant?”

Here’s the worst part.

Nothing worked.

The login didn’t work.

The dashboard didn’t work.

The database didn’t exist.

But billing?

Billing worked perfectly.

Accidental billing is apparently the strongest force in software.

At the end of the day, I told him:

“AI can help you code faster. But you still need authentication, testing, database rules, backups, security, and basic human fear.”

He looked thoughtful.

For a second, I thought I reached him.

Then he said:

“Can I just prompt that?”

And that was the moment a small part of me logged out forever.

AI makes everyone faster.

Including people running in the wrong direction.

And vibe coding is powerful…

…but at some point, the vibes need a database.
